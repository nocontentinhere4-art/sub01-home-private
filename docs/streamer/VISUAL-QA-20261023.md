# SUB-01 streamer visual QA, 2026-10-23 (game k, pid 1488850, build v19)

Visual QA of the live native streamer scene. It covers live tuning of the knobs that need no rebuild, a capture matrix, a 10 s motion clip, and a ranked bug list for the native worker. The native worker is building the next version (hands IK, typing posture, collar/LOD, key light, second camera). No C++ was edited, nothing was rebuilt, and the game and the agent were never restarted. Kimi (kimi-k3.1-pro) reviewed the frames; its findings are marked **[Kimi]** below.

All evidence is on the pod under `/tmp/sub01-share/qa/` (mode 644). Local copies are in the QA worker's scratchpad under `qa/frames/qa/`. The full Kimi question and answer are in `docs/streamer/kimi/q-visual-qa.txt` and `docs/streamer/kimi/visual-qa.md`.

> **WARNING for the next build.** On this skeleton, every "pitch" offset written as `FRotator(Pitch,0,0)` on Spine1, Spine2 and Head rotates about the bone's long axis. That means it twists or turns the bone; it does not bend it (bug 3). The live tuning file therefore holds `posture_head_deg = 14` as a **yaw** correction (it turns his face to the lens) and `posture_lean_deg = 0`. When the axis is fixed, **reset `posture_head_deg` to 0, or move the value to a new yaw key, in `/home/unreal/.sub01-private/streamer-visual-face-tuning.json`**. If you skip this, the same +14 becomes a 14° nod down. The stream pass's `typing_lean_deg` / `typing_head_deg` feed the same broken channels, so they will twist the torso and turn the head instead of sitting him up.

### Measurement method
- **Hair top**: auto-detected as the topmost row with ≥12 consecutive pixels of luma < 32 inside the central 70% of the frame.
- **Eye line and chin**: read by eye on a crop with a 2%-of-height grid overlay (`python3 /tmp/sub01-share/qa/qa2-measure.py out.jpg frame.jpg ...`, runs on the pod with Pillow 11). The eye line is the pupil centres; the chin is the lowest point of the jaw contour.
- **"Head %"** = (chin − hair top) / 720. Reading precision is about ±1%.
- Each setting uses 4 frames, 2 s apart, because the reading loop sways the head ±6 cm.
- The face camera re-samples its aim at every selection (bug 7), which adds about ±2% of vertical noise between runs.
- Pitch and distances come from the game's `SUB01_STREAMER_VISUAL_FACE_CAMERA` log lines and from receipt sampling at 5 Hz.

**The reviewed targets conflict with each other.** This character has a large hair volume and a reading head-down pose, so his eyes sit ~65% of the way from the hair top to the chin. That has two consequences:
- "head 24–27%" combined with "eyes 35–42%" forces a ~20% top margin;
- "8–12% top margin" combined with "eyes 35–42%" forces a ~40% head.

The kept setting gives priority to complete hair, a stable eye line in the 35–42% band and a natural head size. Kimi agrees and retires the old combination. Its revised window is **head 32–38%, eyes 37–42%**, and the kept setting falls inside it.

Even a 24–27% head would need the face ~80 cm from the lens at FOV 95–100. The camera cannot go behind the glass, and the keyboard reaches the desk front edge at seat ~40. The measured head-bone-to-lens distance at seat 30 is 59–65 cm, not the 72 cm the review assumed.

### 1. Hole in the hoodie chest and ragged collar (every face frame)

- **Part**: hoodie front panel and collar; the body under the clothing is not rendered.
- **Cause**: asset/mesh. Candidates: missing or back-facing triangles, a material opacity mask, a decimated LOD, or vertices weighted to bones absent from the 65-bone skeleton. It is not the chair (the backrest is behind him) and not the camera. **[Kimi]** agrees it is an asset problem and separate from the chair penetration.

  2. Toggle two-sided on the hoodie material.
  3. Inspect the hoodie section in Persona in the reference pose with no clip and no procedural layer.
  4. Check for zero-weight vertices or weights to unmapped bones.
  5. Repair the faces, normals and weights, then re-import.

### 2. Head pops at the loop seam of both desk clips, every ~5.5 s
- **Evidence**:
  - reading loop (SupportedWebsite): `qa2-lean0-face-reading-samples.json` and `qa2-lean-16-face-reading-samples.json`. 14 wraps (clip_time 5.2–5.5 → 0.0–0.3) each give a **7.2–7.4 cm** head jump in one 0.2 s sample (+6 cm sideways, +3 back, +3 up), against a median head speed of 3.4 cm/s.
  - typing loop (SupportedDeskTyping): `seq-receipts.jsonl` t = 9.29 → 9.58 and `s95-receipts.jsonl` t = 12.23 give **8.5–8.6 cm**.
  - Visual: `qa2-loop-seam-r95-90-93.jpg` (r95-face-seq frames 90–93: looking at the lens, then 100 ms later bowed and turned right; frame-diff 20.7 against a baseline of 1–2), and `seq-face-seq/frame-0075/0076` (typing seam, diff 22.3).
- **Correction to an earlier observation**: the "one-frame head drop at the reacting entry" seen in `tr3b`/`tr3c` is this seam, not the state entry or the nod. It happens identically with nod 0 (`tr3c-face-seq-sheet-reacting-nod0.jpg`). A controlled run (`r95-*`) shows no pop at the entry (t = 5.27 s), and the pop at the seam (t = 7.5 s).
- **Part**: head and upper body (whole pose).
- **Cause**: clip. The start and end poses of the ~5.5 s loops do not match.
- **Fix**: clip/native. Options:
  - loop crossfade: blend the last 0.3–0.5 s into the start, with inertialization or a two-instance blend;

### 3. The posture, nod and gaze offsets rotate about the wrong axis (twist instead of bend or nod)
- **Evidence**:
  - `qa2-lean{0,-16,16}-face-reading-samples.json`: 40 s each at 5 Hz. The pelvis-to-head line stays **25.2 / 25.0 / 25.4°** forward for lean 0 / −16 / +16, head Y is constant (−298.1 / −297.9 / −298.4), and only ~1 cm of sideways shift appears.
  - `qa2-lean-20/0/20-spectator-reading.jpg` and `qa2-lean±20-monitor-reading.jpg`: the torso **twists** about the vertical.
  - `qa2-head0/20/-20-face-reading.jpg`: posture_head turns the head **left/right**. At +20 he faces the lens; at −20 he is in 3/4 profile.
- **Part**: Spine1, Spine2 and Head local offsets. These are breathing, posture_lean, posture_head, gaze pitch, talking cadence (0.9° at 1.3 Hz), and the 6° "nod" on reacting (`TickStreamerVisualV2` adds to `Motion.Pitch`).
- **Effect**:
  - the sit-up layer does nothing for the forward lean;
  - the reacting **nod is a small head shake**;
  - the talking cadence is a sideways wobble;
  - gaze pitch (−4 / +4.2) turns the head instead of raising or lowering it;
  - the stream pass's typing posture will twist.
- **Cause**: procedural layer. `FRotator.Pitch` maps to the bone's long (twist) axis on the UE-imported Mixamo skeleton.
- **Fix**: native. **[Kimi]**: for each bone, compute the anatomical lateral axis in bone space as `a_local = normalize(inverse(Q_ref) * R_component)`, where R is the character's right vector in component space. Apply a quaternion about `a_local`: spread the lean over the spine chain and put the nod on the neck and head. The axis is likely local X (`Roll`), but verify it rather than swapping channels blindly.
- **Acceptance [Kimi]**:
  - +10° commanded lean changes the pelvis-to-head inclination by 10 ± 1°, with pelvis movement < 1 mm and sideways head drift < 5 mm;
  - +5° nod gives 5 ± 0.5° of face pitch, < 1° of yaw and < 1° of twist.
- Re-run `qa2-lean.sh`; it prints `trunk_fwd_deg`.

### 7. Face-camera aim re-sampled at every selection; the camera arrives ~4 s late
- **Evidence**: across the runs, with identical tuning, the aim varied:
  - aim X: 204.3 – 215.0
  - look pitch: 12.2 – 14.7°
  - face centre: from −55 to +75 px across the matrix

  The cause is that the aim is the mean of a 1 s head window (`FACE_SAMPLE_SECONDS = 1`), and the reading loop sways the head ±6 cm. Every `SUB01_STREAMER_VISUAL_FACE_CAMERA` line shows `settled=0`, so the 2 s fallback is always taken. In `seq-face-seq` frames 30–35 (t = 3.6–4.2 s after selection) the camera was still blending (receipt `face_camera_blend 0.76` at t = 4.1 s).
- **Part**: face camera framing.
- **Cause**: camera code. The aim is re-solved from a short noisy window on every selection; the settle check never passes.
- **Fix**: native.
  - Calibrate the aim once over ≥ 11 s (two loops), store it, and reuse it across selections; re-aim only when the tuning sequence changes.
  - Start the blend at once.
  - Fix the `Settled` comparison.
  - Optionally add a slow critically-damped follow with a time constant ≥ 2 s.
- **Acceptance [Kimi]**: across 20 same-state reselections, aim change < 0.2° and eye line change < 1% of frame height; face camera fully blended ≤ 1.2 s after selection.

## 4. Kimi review (kimi-k3.1-pro, 250 s, 8 frames: original, kept-setting face idle/reading/typing/talking, spectator reading, monitor typing, side view)

**Kimi's ranking**:
1. hoodie chest hole and collar (asset);
2. chair/body penetration and the 25° hinge (placement + clip);
3. typing crown-only;
4. procedural axis, where the nod is a shake, and a "one-frame drop at reacting entry";
5. mouse pantomime;
6. face lighting;
7. headroom and selection-dependent reframing.

**Kimi's judgement of the framing**: head ~30%, eyes ~41% and margin ~22% (the FOV 100 version Kimi saw) is "watchable", but the headroom is generous and FOV 100 reads wider than a typical monitor webcam. Kimi's priority order is:
1. complete hair, unobstructed face and stable framing;
2. eyes 35–42%;
3. natural head size;
4. last, the old margin and head targets, which should be retired.

Kimi suggested trying FOV 92, then 90, with the camera unchanged. It withdrew its 72 cm distance assumption and agreed with the axis diagnosis and the landmark arithmetic. It also said the +14 yaw stop-gap is acceptable if the knob is documented and later replaced.

**Where QA follows Kimi**:
- keeping the hole and the chair penetration as separate bugs;
- the revised framing window (head 32–38%, eyes 37–42%);
- the axis-fix recipe and its numeric checks;
- the acceptance checks used in bugs 3, 5, 6 and 7;
- +1 EV on the face.

**Where QA disagrees, with reasons**:
- **The reacting "one-frame drop" is not an unblended state entry.** Kimi was told about a reacting-entry drop, which was the QA's own first reading. The controlled run (`r95-*`) shows no pop at the entry. The pop sits on the loop seam (bug 2), which happens every ~5.5 s in every desk state, so a 150–250 ms entry blend would not fix it. QA therefore ranks the seam pop #2, above Kimi's #3–#4, because it is the most frequent motion flaw on stream.
- **FOV 92/90.** Tested: FOV 92 gives head ~36%, eyes ~40%, hair top ~16.5%, which is no better than FOV 95 (head ~35%, eyes ~39%, hair top ~16%). The owner asked for a wider view with the room visible, and FOV 95 is the reviewed value and inside Kimi's window. QA keeps FOV 95 rather than 90–92.
