# SUB-01: progress update, 24 October 2026

SUB-01 is a simulated 20-year-old streamer who lives in a 3D house built in Unreal Engine 5. He sits at his desk, uses
a real Windows PC, talks to his chat in English with his own synthetic voice, and carries out what the chat asks him
to do. Two cameras film him, and an automatic director composes and records the stream.

This page sums up the work so far, with photos of how things looked before and how they look now. Everything is still
in private testing: nothing is live yet. Every design decision is taken together with a second AI reviewer (Kimi),
which also reviews every change before it is saved.

## What has been done

**The house and the streamer mode**
- The house runs as an Unreal Engine 5 game on a GPU server.
- In streamer mode he stays at his desk. His other routines (phone, walks, sleep, TV, going out) are paused, not
  removed.

**SUB-01 at his desk: posture, hands, mouth**
- 23/10: seated animations (reading, typing, talking, reacting), breathing, gaze towards the screen, and state changes
  driven by the AI agent.
- 23/10, evening:
  - hands on the keyboard and the mouse;
  - posture, nods and gaze rotate on the right axes: before, they twisted instead of bending;
  - a smooth blend where the animation loops: before, his head jumped about every 5.5 seconds;
  - the webcam aims once and then stays still.
- 24/10:
  - the chair fits his back; before, the backrest went 22 cm into his back;
  - the keyboard follows the chair;
  - a key light lights only him.
- Mouth: the voice produces a 25 Hz signal from the audio. The 3D model has no face bones yet, so for now the signal
  only moves his head slightly in time with his speech.

**Two cameras**
- Camera 1 is the frontal webcam, now with a wider framing: his head fills about a third of the frame.
- Camera 2 films him from behind (1280x720). It is captured inside the game and sent to the director.

**Director and recording**
- 60 fps hardware-encoded recording.
- Layouts switched live: webcam, desktop, split screen, room.
- Privacy cut: when a login page is on his screen, the output switches to the webcam only. It is implemented and covered
  by automated tests; the live test is still to come.
- A speed guard restarts the encoder if the recording falls behind real time. It already caught one slow run on its own.

**A real PC**
- SUB-01 uses a real Windows 11 cloud PC with Steam (Detroit: Become Human is installed).
- Keyboard and mouse were tested live: typing and clearing a YouTube search, opening the Start menu, scrolling.
- The AI agent, the director and the watchdog now all use the same desktop source.

**Chat and agent, with safety limits**
- The chat reaches SUB-01's brain, and the agent receives the messages in batches.
- He carries out what he can do (open a site, search, scroll, go back, read) and politely refuses the rest.
- 30-minute trial on 23/10:
  - 51 decisions, 50 actions, none failed, 13 sentences spoken;
  - a browser crash and a model error, both recovered automatically;
  - a spam message and a request for an admin password filtered before reaching him.
- Safety:
  - a second AI follows every decision in shadow, proposing and comparing, without acting;
  - every action is tied to the screen it was decided on, and checked again if the screen changed;
  - rate limits on actions, and an automatic stop after repeated errors;
  - an independent watchdog and a kill switch that stops actions and voice within a second;
  - a log of every action;
  - he never reveals or types secrets on screen; payments and adult content are blocked.
- The agent stays paused until all of these checks pass on the final setup.

**Voice**
- His voice (K07) speaks English and is generated on the server's GPU. It is synthetic: it is not anyone's real voice.

**Frame rate**
- The game first ran at 15-20 fps: the server limits the CPU each machine can use, and the game was being throttled.
  Spreading the work across dedicated CPU cores brought it to 57-58 fps without the second camera.
- Shadows turned out not to be the bottleneck: turning them all off saves less than 1 ms per frame.
- A slow periodic drift was found: the render thread gets about 0.5 ms slower every minute and resets about every 7
  minutes. It explains the swings of about 5 fps. Its cause is under investigation: it is not the memory allocator, nor
  the in-game monitor's image feed, nor load from other machines on the server.

## Measured numbers

| What | Value | When |
|---|---|---|
| Game before the CPU fix | 15-20 fps | 23/10 |
| Game without the second camera | 57-58 fps (59.2 uncapped) | 24/10 |
| Game with the second camera at 30 fps | main view 59.7-59.9 fps; second camera about 27 fps | 24/10 |
| Game with the second camera at 60 fps | 45-48 fps; second camera 27-43 fps | 24/10 |
| Recording | 60 fps, in real time, with and without the second camera | 24/10 |
| Voice | 2.8-5.1 s to generate a 3-6 s sentence | 23/10 |
| 30-minute agent trial | 51 decisions, 50 actions, 0 failed, 13 sentences, 2 errors recovered | 23/10 |

The target is 60 fps on everything with both cameras at full rate. **It is not reached yet**: running the second camera
at 30 fps keeps the main view smooth in the meantime, but it does not count as the target.

## Next steps

1. **60 fps with both cameras at full rate**, starting from the periodic drift on the render thread.
2. **The monitor inside the game**: show his PC screen in the game at a smooth frame rate. Today it updates about twice
   a second and appears white.
3. **Visual fixes**:
   - the second camera's overexposure;
   - the stretched hoodie at the right elbow;
   - the seam between neck and hood;
   - a straighter, more natural posture;
   - hands that really touch the keyboard and mouse;
   - mouth movement: the model needs a jaw.
4. **The real PC as the single source for everything**, including the monitor inside the game.
5. **Final checks of the AI agent's safety limits**, then a 30-minute trial with the second AI in shadow.
6. **A 30-minute acceptance recording** showing a change of activity, a chat request carried out and one refused, a
   recovered error, both cameras, voice with head and mouth movement, control of the PC, and the measured frame rate.
7. **A private test of the player on subzerone.live.**
8. **Going live on subzerone.live.**

Known limits of the first version: the PC's own audio is not recorded, only his voice; the chat comes only from
subzerone.live.
