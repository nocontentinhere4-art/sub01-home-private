NOTE FOR THE WARM DELIVERY BELOW 59.5 FPS (from the tap's design and the cam2 analysis; nothing measured by me)
- A sequence is given only when a capture is ACCEPTED (after the reader gate, the main_fps rate gate and a free ring slot), and each
  accepted sequence ends written or in exactly one drop counter. So in the director's seq tap / the probe: unique fps < 59.5 with
  seq.missing ~0 means fewer captures (render calls below 60/s, rate_skipped, or dropped_slots: those never get a sequence); missing > 0
  means post-acceptance drops, and the main.json counter that grows says where (deltas over 60 s, main.json is cumulative):
  - dropped_slots: the 3-slot ring was full: encoders behind (2 encoders: encode_ms must stay well under 33 ms) or late GPU copies.
  - dropped_queue: the 2-frame output queue overflowed: the writer is blocked on a full pipe = the READER is behind. Same mechanism as
    cam2's dropped_full (52-53 of 60 offscreen, ~7 drops/s at 1 MiB): reader back-pressure, not the game.
  - dropped_stalled / frames_discarded_midwrite (+ MAIN_TAP_CUT lines): the reader stalled for the whole 0.5 s deadline.
  - dropped_noreader: the reader closed or was away; dropped_encode: failed encodes; dropped_format: a back buffer that is not 1920x1080.
- Pipe slack: 1 MiB (pipe_size in main.json) holds only ~4-6 main frames at 170-250 KB each, ~70-100 ms. A reader that pauses longer
  (ffmpeg 6.1's main thread demuxing/decoding 1080p MJPEG next to cam2, the filters and NVENC; its "Thread message queue blocking"
  warnings at run start) back-pressures the writer exactly as cam2 showed. Cheap checks: main.json deltas with the director reading
  vs the probe reading (the probe drains as fast as it can); the director's seq-tap gaps vs main.json `written`; main_quality 75
  (fewer bytes per frame, same 60 fps) as an A/B; a larger pipe needs root's /proc/sys/fs/pipe-max-size (the tap asks for the max).
