1. **GPU: plausible, not guaranteed.** VRAM starts at **12–13 GB** (9 + 3–4), leaving 11–12 GB for capture targets, CUDA/NVENC contexts and driver overhead. UE’s 60-fps budget is **16.67 ms**; 15% utilisation under a 24-fps cap proves little. One 1080p60 NVENC encode should be reasonable, but x11grab, MJPEG/PiP and software browsers may be CPU-bound. A 720p BGRA readback is **3.69 MB/frame, 111 MB/s at 30 fps**: synchronisation and the extra scene render matter more than PCIe bandwidth.

   **Tuning order:** (a) use asynchronous readback with a 2–3-frame ring, worker-thread JPEG and stale-frame dropping—never a synchronous GPU flush; render SceneCapture only at 30 Hz with minimal show flags; (b) if spikes correlate with TTS, serialize it, cap concurrency and pre-render reusable lines; (c) if GPU-bound, cheapen SceneCapture, then UE dynamic resolution/scalability—Lumen, shadows, post-processing; (d) if CPU-bound, reduce ffmpeg conversion/PiP and browser work. Last resorts are lower capture rate/resolution or moving TTS, provided another device meets real time.

   Log per-frame UE Game/Draw/RHI/GPU p50/p95/p99 (`stat unit`, `profilegpu`), readback/queue/JPEG latency and drops, TTS latency/concurrency, ffmpeg fps/drops, per-core CPU/RAM, and VRAM, SM/encoder/decoder/PCIe utilisation, clocks, power and throttling.

Kimi’s 3–10 s latency may fit a 30 s loop but can make actions stale in a 10 s loop; reasoning tokens alone determine neither quality nor total cost. Compare full-response p50/p95/max latency/timeouts; click-within-target and exact-action success, split by small text; first-pass JSON validity/retries and unsafe/hallucinated actions; image/input/output/reasoning tokens and current-price **cost per correct action**. 3. **Before unattended operation, highest risk first:**
   - **Blast radius:** Prefer a disposable VM. On the physical PC, use a dedicated non-admin Windows account/browser profile, with no personal sessions, payment methods, password store, shared clipboard/drives or saved credentials; restrict network egress. Fail closed on sensitive windows before uploading screenshots. If you cannot isolate it, do not run unattended.
   - **Deny-by-default broker:** The model gets no raw desktop-control credentials. Strictly validate action enums, key/URL/window allowlists, text limits and transformed coordinate bounds. Block shell/clipboard/file transfer and OS shortcuts. Require workflow-specific, out-of-band human approval for purchases, sending/posting, deletion, or credential/settings changes.
   - **Fresh-state/focus interlock:** Bind decisions to screenshot/frame ID and input epoch. Immediately before dispatch, re-check foreground target, URL, resolution and freshness; fail closed on human input, focus/stream changes or staleness. Re-screenshot after consequential actions, deduplicate decision IDs, and never blindly retry a timed-out click.
   - **Independent stop/watchdog:** A separate-process kill switch must stop injection and release keys. Initially cap at **3 actions/decision, 20 actions/minute, 3 consecutive failures, and a 60-minute lease**. Frozen/black video or missed heartbeat means stop and require manual re-arm.
   - **Audit/staging:** Keep append-only logs of frames/hashes, raw JSON, validated actions, targets/results and timestamps, plus session recording. Run supervised dry-runs, then a canary; no fallback path may bypass these checks.
