You are reviewing a performance decision for SUB-01, a native Unreal Engine 5.8.3 game (Vulkan, Linux, NVIDIA RTX PRO 4000
Blackwell, driver 595) running in a RunPod container. A seated streamer character is filmed by the main game viewport
(the "webcam", 1920x1080) and a second camera (cam2: USceneCaptureComponent2D, 1280x720, async readback ring of 3
FRHIGPUTextureReadback, JPEG encoded on a background task, written to a FIFO for ffmpeg). Owner requirement: 60 fps

PROPOSED PLAN (v21 build, ~25 min incremental; one game relaunch ~8 min; the running game must stay up until the new
one is verified):

     siblings); a small helper `streamer_cpu_partition.py` pins every other process of the game's user to cores 6-9 and
     re-applies every 10 s for new processes. Total stays <= 10 cores -> no quota throttling. -corelimit=6.
 P2. Instrumentation in v21: PERF adds game_ms, render_ms, rhi_ms, swap_ms (GSwapBufferTime), render_wait_ms; a live
     console channel: a "console" list in the private tuning file (uid 1001, 0600, growing sequence) executed once per
     sequence through GEngine->Exec, only for whitelisted prefixes (r., sg., t.MaxFPS, csvprofile, fx., a., foliage.)
     so I can test r.* cvars live without relaunching.
 P3. cam2 costs nothing without a reader: no CaptureSceneDeferred, readback or JPEG encode while the FIFO has no reader
     (open attempt every 1 s; today it captures and encodes every frame and drops them at the writer).
 P4. -RenderOffScreen + an engine-side main-view feed: on OnBackBufferReadyToPresent the back buffer is copied into the
     same kind of async readback ring and written as RAW BGRA 1920x1080 frames (8.3 MB, fixed size, whole frames

     encode measures 11 ms) = 1.5 cores at 60 fps; raw costs two 8 MB copies per frame on each side.
     Launch m windowed first (P1+P2+P3 only) or straight with -RenderOffScreen + P4?
